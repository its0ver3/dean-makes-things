Building ERSIM - a 911 call taker training tool

August 16, 2026

I started working on this project with my cousin, who works at a 911 call taking centre. We were out for dinner and I was telling him about Virtual Patient. He mentioned that something like that could be cool for his line of work, especially for training new call takers.

I already had the bones of it from Virtual Patient. I could just make the scenario different, add in an ElevenLabs agent for the voice and adjust the feedback. Turns out that worked pretty well.

The project is called ERSIM, or Emergency Response Simulator. During a call, a trainee has to figure out the caller's location, get the tombstone information, and choose the right file type. Afterwards it looks at those things, as well as the overall call quality and how they handled the situation.

The initial version was very similar to Virtual Patient. There were case cards, you would pick a scenario and difficulty, run through the call, and get a grade. It worked, but I started to think that it was a bit too open-ended for this kind of product.

I came up with the idea of pathways instead. Rather than dropping people into any scenario, it is a curated progression where you build on skills one at a time. You can start with easier calls, work on a specific part of the job, and then move on once you have that down.

I also tried a completely different UI. I turned it into a top-down, sprite-based 2D game that felt a bit like Stardew Valley. The guts of it were the same: you would take calls, get feedback, and improve. But instead of clicking through pathways and case cards, you had a character walking around and interacting with the world before starting calls.

That was a fun thing to experiment with. It could make sense as a game or a more mass-market product, almost like an escape room. But I think the professional training tool makes the most sense right now. The ideal customer is an organization that is hiring people and wants to train them faster while getting better-quality hires.

We have shown it to real call taker trainers and people who are in training, and the reception has been really good. Right now it is a live prototype and we are focused on building the rubric and learning curriculum that will make it as useful as possible.

As a business, I think it will take a while to take hold. There are companies in the States doing things like this, but I have not seen much in Canada. I think the industry is moving in a direction where this could become a really valuable tool.

More than anything, this project took what I learned from Virtual Patient and put it into a completely new context. I learned a ton from it, tried a bunch of different ideas, and felt like it moved my skills forward.
